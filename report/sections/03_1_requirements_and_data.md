# 3. Procedure and achievement of the course project

## 3.1 Requirements analysis, data set and preprocessing

### Requirements

The task is given by the course project guides [2]. It is to design a star-schema data warehouse for flight journeys whose fact is the *flight segment fact table*. The supplied data must be preprocessed (in particular, stopover information is created from transit airports), and the warehouse is built on SQL Server. Finally, at least seven OLAP operations are applied, each with three different MDX statements, and the stopovers are analysed.

### Source data set (Group 2)

The Group 2 fact table `Fact_Airticket` has **600 rows** and 14 columns (price range, departure / transit / landing airport, departure and arrival date, passenger, ticket, plane, cabin, mileage, price, rate and a row id `ian`). It is described by eight dimension tables: airlines (18 rows), airports (184), dates (365 days of 2021), passengers (about 62,000), planes (18), price ranges (30), seats (1,353) and tickets (568,917 flight instances with flight number and times).

### Preprocessing

A deterministic Python pipeline (fixed random seed, repeatable) transforms the data. Its main rules:

1. **One route per flight number.** The ticket table has no airports, so every flight number receives one departure, one landing and an optional stopover airport, and all its tickets and passengers inherit them. Only airports that exist in the airport dimension and have an airport code are used.
2. **Stopovers.** A stopover is given to flights whose direct route is at least 900 km. The transit airport differs from both ends, lies at least 150 km from each, and the detour is at most 25% longer than the direct distance. Landing and take-off times of the stopover are not modelled, as required.
3. **Dates.** Whole ticket groups are moved to other tickets of the same flight number so that flights spread across eight months of 2021; arrival dates come from the ticket's arrival time (overnight flights are handled).
4. **Airlines.** Four airlines already present in the airline dimension (3U, SC, ZH, 9C) receive 240 supplemental tickets flagged `IsSupplemented = 1`, so that 11 airlines appear. The original 568,917 tickets are untouched.
5. **Aircraft and cabins.** One aircraft per flight number, chosen by route length; cabin classes mixed 70% economy, 22% business, 8% first, with first and business only on aircraft of at least 100 seats.
6. **Mileage and price.** Mileage is the great-circle distance in km (including the stopover leg). Price = full fare x the original discount rate, where the full fare depends on cabin class and tapers with distance, anchored on the medians of the source data.
7. **Data repair.** The nine fact rows whose passenger is missing from the passenger table were re-assigned to existing, otherwise unused passengers; no passenger rows were invented.
